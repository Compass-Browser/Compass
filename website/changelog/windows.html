26 November 2026 · Chromium 157.0.8103.51

  • Compass in Italian: with Italian as your system’s language, Compass’s own menus, sidebar, command bar, settings and bubbles are in Italian too, not just the parts that come from Chromium. Other languages still see Compass’s own text in English.

  • Site settings straight to the settings window: Site settings in the site information popup opens the settings window, without a blank tab first.
  • A translucent sidebar: a space’s translucency, in its theme editor, lets what’s behind the window show through the sidebar and the frame, blurred with Windows 11’s Acrylic.
  • File extensions in the downloads folder: the Downloads tab shows files’ extensions and icons correctly.
  • A wider settings window, so every pane’s name fits in the toolbar.

20261125.2215 Stable

25 November 2026 · Chromium 157.0.8103.51

On stable: everything in the builds below, plus reorganized settings with an About pane, importing from Ardia, Cra, Crest and Yab, sync switches for the whole browser and PlayReady on Windows 11.

  • Settings, reorganized: each pane keeps its switches and short rows up front, and moves the long parts to pages of their own, a row away: Sync and Import in Profiles, the connection, Claude in the sidebar, files and memory in AI, saved passwords, cards and addresses in Passwords, fonts in General, and development mode in Advanced. Each row says how things stand (“Off”, “1 site”, a count), and every pane opens with a line on what it’s for.
  • An About pane: Compass’s version, updates and update channel moved from Advanced to their own pane, the last one in the toolbar.
  • Import one browser at a time: Settings › Profiles › Import from another browser lists the browsers on your computer with their own icons; pick one to choose how it comes in and what comes over, then see what was imported.
  • Import from Ardia, Cra, Crest and Yab: Arc’s open-source successors are on the import page too. Their spaces become Compass spaces with their names, icons and colors, with pinned tabs, folders, splits and favorites in place, plus history (and passwords from Ardia and Cra). Each can also come in as a new profile.
  • Sync switches for the whole browser: what syncs (passwords, history, bookmarks and the rest) is now one set of switches for every profile on this device, instead of one per profile.
  • PlayReady on Windows 11: streaming services that use Microsoft’s PlayReady DRM play through Windows’ own hardware-secure decoder, with nothing to download.

20261124.2306 Canary

25 November 2026 · Chromium 157.0.8103.51

  • Your own free sync server on Cloudflare: no server? On the Sync page, choose “Set up a free one on Cloudflare”, sign in with Cloudflare (or paste an API token) and Compass puts a sync server in your own Cloudflare account and starts syncing to it. Compass keeps no Cloudflare token.
  • Invite friends to your server: under People on your server, make an invite link, see who uses the server and remove anyone. A friend chooses “I have an invite” on their Sync page and pastes the link. When Compass has a newer version of the server, the same section offers to update it.
  • Phones wake up for your server: a server on Cloudflare can send tabs to iPhones and Android phones and have them sync in the background through the Compass push relay, which keeps no tokens and no logs. You choose this when you set up the server, and can turn it off later under People on your server.
  • Gmail on its tab: a Gmail tab shows your unread count in the sidebar, even while unloaded, and its hover card shows the newest unread messages.
  • Click in hover cards: rows in a tab’s hover card (Gmail, Calendar, GitHub and plugins’ previews) now take clicks: a click opens that item in the tab, and Ctrl-click or a middle click opens it in a new background tab.
  • Join meetings from Calendar’s preview: a Google Calendar row opens the event’s Meet, Zoom or Teams meeting when it has one, else the event. Built-in plugins now use your session on their site without asking; turn that off in Settings › Plugins.

20261124.1123 Stable

24 November 2026 · Chromium 157.0.8103.51

On stable for the first time on Windows: everything in the builds below, plus settings in a window of their own, Sign in with ChatGPT, queued messages in the Claude chat, Zap elements, full-page screenshots, live easel captures and exporting spaces.

  • Settings in a window of their own: every way into settings opens one window per profile, with a toolbar of panes (General, Profiles, Spaces, Links, Shortcuts, Boosts, Plugins, AI, Privacy, Passwords and Advanced) and search, in Compass’s look. Chrome’s own settings are in it too, among them passwords, addresses and cards, languages, search engines and Safety check.
  • Every profile and every space in settings: Settings › Profiles lists all your profiles, not just the window’s; rename any of them, make a new one or delete one. The new Spaces pane lists each profile’s spaces, where you can change a space’s name, icon, theme, profile, archive delay and sync, add spaces, reorder them and delete them.
  • Sign in with ChatGPT: the OpenAI provider can use your ChatGPT plan instead of an API key. Choose Continue with ChatGPT in Settings › AI; the chat, the sidebar and Ask work as with a key.
  • Queued messages in the Claude chat: what you send while Claude is replying waits in a queue and goes once the reply is done, one message at a time.
  • One MCP connection for every profile: an MCP client such as Claude Code now works in every profile that allows MCP clients, without reconnecting from another profile’s window. Each profile asks for its own approval, and profiles that haven’t allowed it show only as a line in the tab list.
  • Zap elements: choose “Zap elements on this site…” in the command bar, then click what you want gone. Undo brings back the last one, Done or Enter keeps them, Esc puts them all back. They’re saved as a Boost named “Zapped on …”, so they sync and you can edit them in Settings › Boosts.
  • Screenshots of a region or the full page: choose Capture Screenshot… in the command bar or the page’s context menu, drag a region or pick Full Page, then copy it or save it to Downloads as a PNG.
  • Live captures on easels: while an easel is open, page captures older than an hour are taken again, so the board shows what the pages look like now.
  • Export spaces: Settings › Spaces saves every space (with your favorites), or one space from its details, as a bookmarks file any browser can import. Pinned tabs and folders become folders, and open tabs go in a “Today” folder.
  • Development mode on localhost: a local development server gets development mode without asking, as in Arc. Remove localhost from the list, or turn development mode off on the page, to keep it off.
  • Import another browser as a profile: a Chrome, Edge, Brave, Vivaldi or Dia profile can now come in as a new Compass profile, cookies and all, as Arc’s can; its sidebar becomes the new profile’s spaces. Choose it beside “Add as a space here” when you import.
  • Actions first in the command bar: type an action’s name (“settings”, “new space”, “copy”) and it comes first, ahead of the address or search, so Enter runs it.

20261122.1521 Canary

22 November 2026 · Chromium 157.0.8103.51

  • A built-in ad blocker: Compass blocks ads, trackers and cookie banners on every site with EasyList, EasyPrivacy and the EasyList Cookie List, the same lists as on iPhone and iPad. It also hides the empty boxes ads leave. Turn it off for a site from the site information popup (or in that site’s permissions, as Ads), or for every site in Settings › Privacy.
  • Your own filter lists: in Settings › Privacy › Filter lists, turn each list on or off, see how many rules it has and when it last updated, and add any Adblock Plus or uBlock Origin list by its address. Lists update once a day; Update Now checks at once. Scriptlets and some uBlock Origin rules aren’t supported, so an extension like uBlock Origin still does more, and both can run together.

20261121.1830 Beta

21 November 2026 · Chromium 157.0.8103.51

Ctrl+Tab in recently used order, tab previews and notification counts, plugins, sharing, and development mode.

  • Switch tabs in recently used order: Ctrl+Tab goes back to the tab you used before this one, as in Arc; press Tab again while holding Ctrl to go further back (Ctrl+Shift+Tab goes the other way), and let go to switch. Hold Ctrl a moment and cards with each tab’s preview show over the window; press W to close the picked tab (Ctrl+Z brings it back). It covers the space you’re in and your favorites. Prefer going down the sidebar? Turn off “Ctrl+Tab goes to recently used tabs” in Settings › Keyboard shortcuts.
  • Tab previews in the sidebar: hover a tab and its card shows what’s on the page instead of a screenshot. A Google Calendar tab shows your next events (calendars in English for now), a GitHub tab your latest notifications. Plugins can add previews for other sites; they read the page apart from it and can’t send what they read anywhere.
  • Notification counts on tabs: a GitHub tab shows how many notifications you haven’t read, as a count at the end of its row (a dot on a favorite), kept current in the background. To see the count and the notifications from any GitHub page, turn on “Advanced: use your session on github.com” for GitHub in Settings › Plugins; without it, the tab shows a dot when something is unread.
  • Settings › Plugins: every live folder and tab preview plugin in one place, with the sites each one reads, its own settings, its source, and Install and Remove.
  • Claude can make plugins: with “Let Claude make plugins” on in Settings › Claude, right-click a tab and choose Make a Preview for…, or describe a live folder to Claude. It looks at the page, tests the plugin on your tab, and asks before saving it; you can undo it in the chat.
  • Development mode, for web developers: press Ctrl+Shift+D on a site you’re working on (or use its switch in the site information popup, the command bar, or Settings › Development Mode). On that site, reload always skips the cache, a bar above the page shows the full address (which you can edit) with buttons for the Console, the Network panel, picking an element and copying the address, and the Network panel lists the requests made before DevTools opened.
  • Share spaces, folders and easels with other people: right-click a pinned folder and choose Share…, choose Share space… in a space’s menu, or Share Easel… on an easel’s tab. Invite people as editors or viewers with a link (it can expire or be limited to a number of people), or by email on a server with company sign-in. Everyone sees the same tabs and boards, end-to-end encrypted: the sync server can’t read them. Opening an invite link in Compass shows what it is and lets you pick the space to put it in.
  • Company sign-in for sync servers: a self-hosted sync server can ask for a sign-in with your company’s identity provider (OIDC). Compass shows “Sign in to … to sync” and a code to check, and remembers the sign-in for the chain. Servers without it work as before.
  • Install a .crx from its developer: with Developer mode on in chrome://extensions, a .crx opened from a link installs like one dropped on that page (uBlock Origin’s GitHub builds, for example). With Developer mode off, the message now says to turn it on.
  • Fixed: saving a key for OpenAI or another provider no longer erases your Anthropic key, endpoint and model.
  • Fixed: a call could stay out of picture-in-picture when you left its tab right after a video tile turned.
  • Fixed: menus in the Share sheet closed as soon as they opened.
  • Fixed: a new profile could stay at “Starting…” forever when syncing with a server that lists its allowed chains.
