Compass Browser Privacy Policy

Effective 17 November 2026

In short: Compass Browser has no ads, no analytics and no tracking. What you browse stays on your iPhone, iPad or Android device. If you turn on sync, your data is encrypted on your devices before it leaves them, and the sync server can't read it.

Who this covers

This policy covers the Compass Browser app for iPhone and iPad and its password AutoFill extension, the Compass Browser app for Android and its password autofill service (together, "the app"), and the Compass sync service run by the developer of the app ("we"). The sections below describe the iPhone and iPad app; Compass for Android says where the Android app differs.

What stays on your iPhone or iPad

The app stores these only on your device:

  • your spaces, tabs, pinned tabs, favorites and archive;
  • your browsing history (kept for 90 days);
  • cookies, site data and the cache of the websites you visit;
  • saved passwords, in the iOS Keychain;
  • files you download;
  • your settings, including per-site settings.

We don't receive any of it unless you turn on sync (below). You can delete it with Settings → Clear Browsing Data, from each part of the app, or by deleting the app.

What the app doesn't do

  • No advertising, and no ad or tracking SDKs.
  • No analytics or usage statistics sent to us.
  • No account with us: sync uses a key made on your devices, not an email address or a phone number.
  • No selling or sharing of data with anyone.

Sync (optional)

Sync is off until you join it from your Mac or with a recovery code. When it's on, the app syncs your spaces and tabs, history, passwords, open tabs and the list of your devices with Compass on your other devices, through a sync server.

  • End-to-end encryption. Your data is encrypted on your device with a key only your devices have, before it's sent. The server stores it encrypted and can't read it. Without your devices or your recovery code, nobody can decrypt it, including us.
  • What the server can see. To work, the server sees an identifier for your sync chain (derived from your key), the type, size and time of each stored item, and your devices' list entries. A device entry isn't encrypted: it holds the device name as iOS reports it (usually just "iPhone" or "iPad"), its model, the app's version and when it last synced, so your other devices can list it.
  • Server logs. The server logs requests that fail, with their time, path and the app's version. It doesn't log IP addresses. Logs are rotated and kept briefly.
  • Your own server. You can use your own sync server instead of ours; then this section's server is yours.
  • Stopping. Settings → Leave Sync stops syncing on this device and keeps your data on it. Your encrypted data on the server stays readable only by your remaining devices.

Services the app contacts

  • Websites you visit, as any browser does. They see your IP address and what they normally see, under their own privacy policies.
  • Your search engine. What you search for in the address bar goes to the search engine you choose in Settings (Google by default).
  • Filter list publishers. To block ads, trackers and cookie banners, the app downloads public filter lists from easylist.to and secure.fanboy.co.nz, about once a day. These requests carry nothing about you or your browsing.
  • Site icons. The app fetches a site's icon from that site.
  • The sync server, if sync is on (above).

Permissions

  • Camera: only to scan the pairing code shown by Compass on your Mac. The image is processed on your device and never sent anywhere.
  • Face ID or Touch ID: only to unlock your saved passwords and your recovery code. The app never gets your face or fingerprint data; iOS handles it.

Compass for Android

The Android app follows the rest of this policy: no ads, analytics or tracking, no account with us, and end-to-end encrypted sync. It differs in these ways.

  • On your device. Your spaces, tabs, history, cookies and site data, downloads and settings stay on your device, as above. Saved passwords are kept in the app's own password storage on the device.
  • Sync. Sync works as described above, and also syncs your settings, autofill data and the Claude memories below. A device entry holds the device name as Android reports it, which can be a name you gave your phone, its model, the app's version and when it last synced. To stop, leave sync in the app's settings; when the last device of your sync chain leaves, its data is removed from the server.
  • Sent tabs. To show tabs you send from your other devices, the app registers with Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM), Google's push service, through Google Play services, and gives the sync server the token FCM issues. The server sends a wake-up through FCM that says only that a tab is waiting; the tab itself comes by sync, encrypted. Leaving sync removes the token from the server.
  • Passwords in other apps. If you choose Compass as Android's autofill service, it offers your saved passwords in other apps and can save logins you enter there. This happens on your device; the passwords sync only as described above.
  • Claude (optional). If you add your own Anthropic API key, or an endpoint you choose, and ask Claude something, the app sends your question, the text of the page you're on and the results of what Claude does in the page directly to Anthropic or that endpoint, under their terms. It never goes through us. The key stays encrypted on your device. Facts Claude remembers about you (which you can see and delete, or turn off) are kept on your device and sync end-to-end encrypted.
  • Google services in the browser. Like other Chromium-based browsers, the app contacts Google to update and install extensions and the Chrome Web Store (sending the browser's version and your extensions' identifiers), to update browser components such as certificate revocation lists, for Safe Browsing (which checks shortened hashes of web addresses, not the addresses themselves), and to deliver websites' push notifications if you allow them. There's no Google sign-in in the app, and no usage statistics or crash reports are sent.
  • Extensions you install from the Chrome Web Store run under their own privacy policies.
  • Permissions. Location, camera, microphone and contacts are used only when a website asks and you allow it, or to scan a pairing code with the camera (processed on your device). Location is never used in the background. Notifications show downloads, media and sent tabs. Your fingerprint or face unlock (Android's biometric prompt) can lock Incognito tabs and saved passwords; the app never gets your biometric data.
  • Crash reports. Crash dumps stay on your device. If you've allowed Android to share usage and diagnostics with Google, Google Play may show us crash and performance statistics for the app, which don't include your browsing.

Crash reports

If you've allowed it in iOS, Apple may share crash reports with us. They help us fix the app and don't include your browsing. For Android, see Compass for Android.

Children

The app isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect data from them.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update it on this page and change the date at the top.

Contact

Questions about privacy: on iPhone or iPad, send feedback from the app through TestFlight (take a screenshot, then Share Beta Feedback). On Android, write to the contact email on Compass Browser's Google Play page; you can also ask us there to delete your sync chain's data from our server.
